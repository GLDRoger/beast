# Phrases to Remove

Use these as diagnostic examples, not blanket bans or required passes. Preserve meaning, warranted uncertainty, quotations, and the user's intentional voice. Apply only the patterns that weaken the current piece.
Cut these. State the content directly.

## Throat-clearing openers

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]" / "Here's this [X]" / "Here's that [X]" / "Here's why [X]"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "Here's what I find interesting"
- "Here's the problem though"
- "Let's dive in" / "Without further ado" / "First things first"
- "Ever wondered…?" / "Have you ever…?"
- "Look," (sentence-start filler)
- "So," (paragraph-start filler when content-free)

Any "here's what/this/that" construction is throat-clearing. Cut and state the point.

## Insight announcements (honest-prose)

- "Its deepest idea is…"
- "The part I want you to sit with…"
- "Here is the part that made me laugh out loud"
- "Read that again slowly"
- "There is a specific kind of X" (when only used to promise value)
- "What I mean is:" / "Here's what I mean:"

## Emphasis crutches

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"
- "This is genuinely hard"
- "This is what [X] actually looks like"
- "actually matters"

## Business / LinkedIn jargon

| Avoid | Prefer |
|-------|--------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, address |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, do |
| Landscape (context) | Situation, field |
| Ecosystem / space (domain) | The field, the market, the product |
| Game-changer | Name the concrete change |
| Double down | Commit, increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis, look at |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Moving forward | Next, from now |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |
| On the same page | Aligned, agreed |
| Journey / transform / evolve (empty) | What actually changed |
| Holistic / end-to-end / frictionless | Specific claim |
| Battle-tested / production-ready | What failed and what didn't |
| All-in-one / scale without limits | Concrete scope |

## Classic AI lexicon (remove on sight)

*Empty adjectives:* seamless, robust, comprehensive, powerful, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art, groundbreaking, innovative, revolutionary, stellar, formidable, compelling, engaging, captivating, marvelous, paramount, crucial, fantastic, pivotal, vital, essential (when unmeasured)

*AI vocab spikes:* delve, leverage, elevate, intricate, meticulously, synergy, empower, tapestry, testament, beacon, realm, symphony, vibrant, nestled, renowned, showcasing, underscore, hone, unveil, unravel, harness, foster, navigate, tackle, catapult, supercharge, unleash, unlock, craft (vague verb), multifaceted, nuanced (as seasoning)

*Marketing verbs / slogans:* exceed, game-changer, boasts, committed to, moves the needle, secret sauce, magic (product copy), drive impact, unlock value, elevate your [noun], chaos into clarity, build the future of work

## Adverbs and softeners

Kill empty -ly words and intensifiers:

- really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually
- deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, inevitably
- interestingly, importantly, crucially
- arguably, fairly, potentially, possibly (when only hedging)

Also cut:

- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]" / "In today's fast-paced world" / "In a world where"
- "It's worth noting" / "Worth mentioning" / "Keep in mind"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "The reality is"
- "In many ways" / "To some extent" / "In a sense"
- "It is important to note"

## Transition glue

- "Furthermore," / "Additionally," / "Moreover," / "That being said,"
- "However," as paragraph-open crutch
- "In conclusion," / "To summarize," / "Overall," / "In summary,"
- "On the other hand," / "Conversely," used for fake dialectic
- "As mentioned above" / "As previously discussed" / "Building on that"
- "As we'll see…" / "As noted earlier…"

## Meta-commentary

- "Hint:" / "Plot twist:" / "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Dressed up as"
- "The rest of this essay explains…"
- "Let me walk you through…"
- "In this section, we'll…"
- "I want to explore…"
- "The rest of this piece…"

## Performative emphasis / false intimacy

- "creeps in"
- "I promise" / "They exist, I promise"
- "Great question!" / "Absolutely!" / "You're right to ask…"
- "Feel free to…"
- "You might want to consider…"
- "Sorry if this is basic…" / "I may be wrong, but…"
- "As an AI…" / "I don't have personal experience…"
- "What do you think? Let's discuss in the comments!"

## Vague declaratives

Kill or replace with the specific thing:

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"
- "The part everyone gets wrong" / "What most people miss"

## Marketing cadence phrases

- "This doesn't just X — it also Y"
- "It's not about X, it's about Y"
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- "Built from production usage"
- "Your all-in-one platform"
- "Scale without limits"
- Any sentence starting "This skill reads your…" / "This tool analyzes your…" (generic product voice)
