# Essay & long-form craft

Absorbed from honest-prose. Use fully for essays and posts; selectively for docs.

## 1. Never announce the insight before delivering it

The reader does not need to be told a good bit is coming. The good bit does that job.

- ✗ "Its deepest idea is structural. The protagonist is not one person."
- ✓ "It kills its main character. Then it does it again. And again."

- ✗ "Look at how thin the evidence was. In 1965 Gordon Moore had about five points on a graph."
- ✓ "In 1965 Gordon Moore had about five points on a graph."

## 2. Kill the "not X, it's Y" reversal

Loudest AI signature. State Y.

- ✗ "Senku's real superpower is not memory. It is dependency."
- ✓ "Senku's superpower is dependency."

## 3. Do not restate after a visual, scene, or fact

If a scene showed a tin droplet struck by a laser, the next paragraph does not re-describe that droplet. **After a scene, prose picks up where the facts left off, not where the scene left off.**

If an illustration lists eight questions, the caption does not list them again.

## 4. Facts that open unpaid detours → footnote or cut

Ask: *does this fact change what the reader thinks, or does it just prove I did research?*

- Node-name trivia that forces two paragraphs of explanation → footnote or cut.
- "AlphaFold's first version won and was still useless" → if you only need "second attempt," say that in three words.

## 5. Set up before you pay off

If a payoff paragraph must introduce a fact to work, the fact was planted too late. Seed it earlier, one clause, no lecture.

Missing scaffolding reads as confusion. Readers blame themselves and stop.

## 6. Explain the noun the first time you use it

"ASML puts the sequence at hundreds of steps" — who is ASML? What is a wafer?

Name it once, plainly, in the sentence where it first appears. Correct detail is higher than you think: *ASML is the Dutch company, the only firm on Earth that builds EUV lithography machines; one costs about $180 million.* That is not a digression; that is the reader feeling the thing.

## 7. Sell it, don't review it

Critic voice describes a property. Fan voice shows the work.

- ✗ "And then it spends a hundred chapters refusing to let them cheat."
- ✓ Show the chain: sulfa → sulfuric acid → the right rock → copper → smelt → furnace → charcoal…

The recursion *is* the feeling. Show the chain, not the adjective.

## 8. Excitement is in selection, never in labeling

Do not write "isn't this amazing." Write the fact that made you feel it, then let the sentence break.

- ✓ "One company. On the whole planet. Everything you have ever done on a screen routes back through a machine that leaves a warehouse in the Netherlands in 250 boxes."

A writer who interrupts himself because he can't get over the facts reads as enthusiastic. A writer who says "this is so cool" is doing a bit.

## 9. Rhythm is not decoration

Seven paragraphs of one clipped declarative each = stiff slide deck. Vary length. Let one sentence run. Let the short one land *after* the long one.

Reverse failure: comma-chain three ideas so the sharpest fact is buried mid-clause. Give the best thing a full stop.

## 10. Pronouns need antecedents; fragments need subjects nearby

- ✗ "The work on screen survives that. It just sets the right posture toward it." (three pronouns, no antecedent)
- ✓ Keep subject and claim in the same breath when distance would confuse.

## 11. No abstraction may perform a human action

- ✗ "It refuses to flatten a complicated world into either miracle or indictment."
- ✓ "You can love the thing and still ask who pays for it."

## 12. Cut the crutches

Adverbs, hedges, greeting-card lines. **One raised voice per essay.** Emphasize three sentences → emphasize none.

## 13. Fact-check the lines that make you sound smart

The lines you're proudest of are most likely wrong — you optimized them for shape.

Look it up before you polish it. A well-shaped wrong sentence is harder to kill later because you'll be attached to it.

## 14. When rewrites get worse, the paragraph's job is wrong

If every rewrite still opens with throat-clearing, the paragraph is a hedge or apology. Change what it's *for*.

- Failed: prove you noticed the film is a company portrait.
- Works: "Go watch the documentary. It was made with DeepMind's blessing, so keep one eye on who paid for the camera."

## 15. Give the big type to the line that implicates the reader

A pull-quote is not the cleverest sentence. It is the one that turns and points.

- ✗ "Every smooth surface is hiding a century of arguments."
- ✓ "I did not build any of this. I can barely explain it. **I am an inheritor, and so are you.**"

Nobody underlines a clever generalisation. They underline the sentence that puts them in it.

## 16. Your outline is not your prose

Shorthand from planning leaks onto the page:

- "Leverage. New verbs." as a subtitle — note to self, not a reader line.
- Puns that need the writer's head to decode mid-scroll.

If a line needs your private context to decode, it is not written yet.

## 17. Choose the form the argument needs

Bullets let the eye skip. If the point is that *requirements keep opening into more requirements*, a run-on paragraph that buries the reader *is* the argument. A neat list is the opposite.

Use a list when the reader must *find* an item again. Use prose when they must *feel* the weight of all of them.

## 18. Editing someone else's voice

- Restored lines are sacred; fix only breakage.
- Say whether you're fixing wrongness or taste.
- Wrong facts: state the correction, use the better fact.
- Unverified render: say so in one complete sentence.
