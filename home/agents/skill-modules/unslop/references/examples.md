# Before/After Examples

Use these as diagnostic examples, not blanket bans or required passes. Preserve meaning, warranted uncertainty, quotations, and the user's intentional voice. Apply only the patterns that weaken the current piece.
## Example 1: Throat-clearing + binary contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

**Changes:** Removed opener, binary contrast, emphasis crutch.

---

## Example 2: Filler + unnecessary reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

**Changes:** Cut hedging, throat-clearing, permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: Business jargon stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

**Changes:** Eliminated jargon. Core message in six words.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "Speed, quality, cost: pick two."

**Changes:** Single sentence. No performative emphasis. (Prefer colon or period over em dash.)

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning, not productivity."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No scaffolding.

---

## Example 6: Insight announcement (honest-prose)

**Before:**
> "Its deepest idea is structural. The protagonist is not one person."

**After:**
> "It kills its main character. Then it does it again. And again."

**Changes:** Deliver the idea; don't preview it.

---

## Example 7: Not X, it's Y

**Before:**
> "Senku's real superpower is not memory. It is dependency."

**After:**
> "Senku's superpower is dependency."

**Changes:** State Y.

---

## Example 8: Abstraction with false agency

**Before:**
> "It refuses to flatten a complicated world into either miracle or indictment."

**After:**
> "You can love the thing and still ask who pays for it."

**Changes:** Human subject; concrete verb.

---

## Example 9: Critic voice → fan voice

**Before:**
> "And then it spends a hundred chapters refusing to let them cheat."

**After:**
> "Senku wants sulfa drugs, so he needs sulfuric acid, so he needs the right rock. He wants a phone line, so first copper, before copper a way to smelt it, before that a furnace, before that charcoal…"

**Changes:** Show the chain; don't review the property.

---

## Example 10: Vague declarative

**Before:**
> "The implications are significant. The stakes are high. The reasons are structural."

**After:**
> "If this ships late, the enterprise deal dies in Q3. Legal blocked the API key path last time; same path is still in the design."

**Changes:** Name the stake, the actor, the prior failure.

---

## Example 11: Restatement tax

**Before:**
> [Scene shows laser hitting tin droplet]
> "As the laser strikes the tiny droplet of tin…"

**After:**
> [Scene]
> "That splash is the light source for every advanced chip line on Earth."

**Changes:** Continue from consequence, not from re-describing the image.

---

## Example 12: Closing restatement

**Before:**
> "In conclusion, we've seen that alignment is hard, communication matters, and teams that learn faster win. The key is to be intentional."

**After:**
> "Ship the weekly demo. Fire the status meeting."

**Changes:** New, sharp implication — not the article again.
