# Prose cleanup

Preserve the writer's voice, the intended meaning, and useful uncertainty. Edit patterns because they weaken this piece, not because a word appears on a list.

- Deliver the point instead of announcing that an insight is coming. Remove paragraphs that only restate the previous scene, fact, or conclusion.
- Replace empty emphasis, generic claims, and canned contrasts with concrete content. Real distinctions and warranted qualifications should remain.
- Use one term per concept. Prefer active voice when the actor matters and varied sentence rhythm over repeated dramatic fragments or identical paragraph endings.
- Cut apology loops, flattery, redundant headings, narrator commentary, and endings that merely repeat the answer. A short acknowledgment should lead to the corrected result.
- Let specific, supported facts create interest. Do not manufacture customer evidence, anecdotes, metrics, or personal details.
- Preserve the user's deliberate wording, quotations, technical terms, and stylistic choices. Distinguish a factual correction from a personal editorial preference.
- Keep factual certainty calibrated. Do not delete “may,” “approximately,” or a caveat when the evidence requires it. Verify consequential or unstable claims with appropriate sources; stylistic strength is not factual support.

## Form

For UI strings and instructions, prioritize the state, actor, next action, and recovery path. For essays, let the argument determine the structure; set up unfamiliar nouns and claims before their payoff. For marketing, make the offer and its limits concrete without manufacturing urgency or certainty.

Punctuation, sentence length, passive voice, and list size are choices, not numerical gates. Keep them when they serve the piece. Use no scorecard unless the user requests one or a real comparison benefits from it.
