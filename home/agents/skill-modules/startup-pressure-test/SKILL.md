# Startup Pressure Test

Use this business lens when the user asks whether an idea can become a viable business, how to validate demand, or how to find early customers. For creative search, competitions, or technical-demo selection, use the growth workflow's appropriate criteria instead.

Start from the supplied idea and prior context. Ask only for a missing fact that materially changes the assessment. A customer hypothesis can be evaluated as a hypothesis; do not require the user to name a real customer before doing useful analysis or fabricate one yourself.

## Choose the relevant mode

- `pressure-test`: business assumptions, material flaws, and a verdict.
- `problem-validation`: evidence of pain, urgency, current behavior, and willingness to change.
- `competition-map`: direct substitutes, workarounds, switching cost, and credible differentiation.
- `first-10-customers`: a concrete early-customer approach when requested.
- `mvp-plan`: the smallest useful test of the central assumption under the actual constraints.
- `full`: a broader assessment when requested or genuinely useful.

Read only the matching sections of `references/playbooks.md` for deeper examples. Its interview forms, schedules, numerical counts, and templates are optional methods, not prerequisites for every answer.

## Evidence and output

Separate verified facts, customer/business hypotheses, and unknowns. Check current market claims when they matter. Existing behavior competes with the product even when there is no direct competitor. Compliments and hypothetical willingness are weaker evidence than observed behavior.

Rank the most consequential flaws and name the core assumption. Give a recommendation and an appropriate next test; use a scorecard only when it clarifies a comparison and each score has evidence. Do not impose a fixed number of sections, flaws, features, or a two-week schedule regardless of the request.

When the task includes finding better ideas and a candidate fails, revise or replace it within the authorized scope and preserve the requested candidate coverage. A verdict on the failed idea does not complete an unresolved search request.

Manual validation can be the cheapest early test, but choose from the actual business, channels, and constraints. Do not turn a useful early-stage heuristic into a universal ban on automation, advertising, or a technically necessary prototype.
