# Idea search and candidate selection

Use for exploratory concepts, competitions, technical demos, and researched shortlists. Business viability is one possible criterion, not the universal objective.

Keep the actual goal, audience, judging rubric, requested candidate count, constraints, rejected directions, and unresolved objections in view. Follow-up corrections update these criteria. Do not let familiar projects or an early favorite substitute for the user's current problem.

## Generate and screen

- Explore meaningfully different problems or mechanisms. Several names for the same narrow workflow are not several distinct candidates.
- Establish basic usefulness: who faces the problem, what happens today, why the current workaround is insufficient, and what becomes possible or easier.
- Before presenting a researched shortlist, check obvious substitutes, incumbent features, feasibility, and any required rubric/eligibility criteria. Unresearched brainstorming is fine when requested; label it accordingly.
- Separate a compelling demo, a plausible business, and a strong competition fit. Do not reject a demo solely for lacking startup traction or declare a business validated by a striking demo.
- Make unfamiliar dependencies and user-adoption burden explicit. A concept that needs behavior change, hardware, sensitive access, or a new native app must earn that cost under the stated constraints.
- Use scores only when a rubric makes them informative. Tie them to evidence; do not manufacture probabilities of winning, customer demand, or novelty.

## Advance after correction

When evidence disqualifies an idea, retire it and research a replacement within the authorized scope. Preserve the requested coverage: if two of four shortlisted ideas fail, provide two qualifying replacements rather than quietly returning only the survivors. An explicit user decision to narrow the set overrides the original count.

Explain how the replacement addresses the specific failed criterion. Do not repeat the rejected mechanism under a new name. If genuinely unable to fill the set, state the shortfall and the concrete limiting evidence; do not pad it with known failures.

Finish with the requested candidates, a justified recommendation when useful, and the weakest unresolved assumption. Do not end at agreement that the current ideas need more work when the user has already asked you to find better ones.
